Neuro-FLux

A Fully Flexible Wearable CW-NIRS Device for Continuous Cerebral Hemodynamic Monitoring
Master's Thesis Research • Carnegie Mellon University • Advisor: Dr. Jana Kainerstorfer

View Project Details

Project Overview

The Problem

Continuous monitoring of cerebral hemodynamics is critical for early detection of neurological conditions and personalized brain health assessment. Existing modalities—EEG and fMRI—are ill-suited for continuous, real-world use due to complexity, restricted mobility, and reliance on controlled environments. While continuous-wave near-infrared spectroscopy (CW-NIRS) offers a simpler alternative, wearable implementations struggle with maintaining signal quality and stable optical coupling during natural movement.

The Solution

Neuro-FLux is a flexible, wearable CW-NIRS system designed to improve both mechanical conformity and photon detection. The device integrates dual-wavelength LEDs, a large-area photodiode (LA-PD), and embedded acquisition electronics on a fully flexible PCB (~0.3 mm thick). This enables the device to conform to the curvature of the human head, maintaining consistent skin contact and stable optical coupling during motion—addressing the primary failure mode of prior wearable NIRS devices.

Key Results

  • SNR of 36.14 dB at heart-rate band—exceeding a commercial CW-NIRS system (Artinis MRK II, 34.49 dB) under similar conditions
  • Stable 100 Hz operation with optimized acquisition parameters (8-sample averaging, 29.2 µs integration time)
  • Qualitative agreement with FD-NIRS (ISS Imagent) during paced breathing and hyperventilation tasks
  • Time-frequency analysis demonstrating real-time tracking of HR modulation during hyperventilation (0.5 Hz paced breathing)
  • Fully flexible form factor: 56.6 × 24.4 mm, ~0.3 mm thick polyimide PCB with TPU enclosure and silicone skin interface

My Role

Lead hardware and firmware engineer for the 2-year Master's thesis project. Designed the flexible PCB (4-layer, impedance-controlled), developed embedded firmware for the STM32WB5MMG MCU with BLE, implemented the acquisition sequence and signal processing pipeline in MATLAB, conducted all experimental validation against commercial NIRS systems, and authored the thesis.

Technical Details

Hardware Architecture

  • Flexible PCB: 4-layer polyimide, ~0.3 mm thick, patterned copper planes for flexibility and noise isolation
  • Optical Sources: Dual-wavelength LEDs (735 nm / 850 nm, SMT735D/850D) in single package
  • Source-Detector Distances: 10 mm (superficial), 27/32/37 mm (cortical)
  • Large-Area Photodiode: PDB-C110 (Advanced Photonix), ~300 pF junction capacitance
  • Analog Front End: MAX86171 (Analog Devices) with adjustable PD biasing for LA-PD stability
  • MCU: STM32WB5MMG with integrated BLE for wireless operation
  • Power: 3.7 V LiPo, 150 mAh, regulated 2.5 V VCC with separate LED/PD domains

Mechanical Design

  • Enclosure: TPU 3D-printed case with designed weak points for bending
  • Headband Interface: 25 mm elastic band slots with tightening mechanism
  • Silicone Skin Interface: Molded compliant pad adapting to hair/skin contours, reducing air gaps
  • Form Factor: 56.6 × 24.4 mm footprint, wearable as headband

Acquisition System

  • Frame Rate: 100 Hz (10 ms/frame)
  • Channels: 8 LED channels (2 wavelengths × 4 SD distances)
  • Per-Channel: 8 samples averaged, 29.2 µs integration time
  • Ambient Light Cancellation: Coarse analog + fine digital (pre/post LED exposure)
  • Settling Time: 6.1 µs (optimized for timing stability)
  • Frame Duration: ~5.7 ms active, remainder low-power

Signal Processing Pipeline

Raw intensity → Ambient-corrected intensity → ΔOD via ln(I/I₀) → Δμₐ via Modified Beer-Lambert Law (MBLL) with literature DPF values → Linear system solve for Δ[HbO] and Δ[Hb] using extinction coefficients at 735/850 nm. Longest SD distance (37 mm) used for cerebral sensitivity.

Validation & Results

Experimental Protocol

  • Session 1: Neuro-FLux vs. ISS Imagent (FD-NIRS) on forehead, bilateral placement
  • Session 2: Neuro-FLux vs. Artinis MRK II (CW-NIRS), same protocol, separate day
  • Tasks: 2-min rest → 3× paced breathing (0.3 Hz) → 3× hyperventilation (0.5 Hz)
  • Analysis: Frequency-domain SNR at HR band (0.8–2 Hz); STFT spectrograms (30 s windows, 75% overlap)

Quantitative Results

Device SD Distance SNR @ HR (dB)
Neuro-FLux 37 mm 36.14
Artinis MRK II 38 mm 34.49

Neuro-FLux achieved comparable and slightly higher signal quality than a commercial CW-NIRS system, attributed to the LA-PD's enhanced photon collection at longer SD distances.

Optimization Findings

Parameter Sweep Results

  • SNR increases with sample averaging and integration time
  • Degradation at max parameters (16 samples, 117 µs) due to frame timing violations
  • PD settling time (6.1 vs 18.1 µs) had no significant SNR impact
  • 50 Hz vs 100 Hz: no substantial SNR difference

Power & Battery

  • 50 Hz: 97.56 mAh/hr → ~1.5 hr battery life
  • 100 Hz: 100.72 mAh/hr → ~1.5 hr battery life
  • Sampling rate has minimal impact on energy consumption

Discussion & Future Work

Significance

This work demonstrates that high-quality optical measurements are achievable in a compact, fully flexible form factor without sacrificing performance. By combining a flexible PCB, LA-PD, and embedded acquisition, Neuro-FLux addresses the key limitation of current wearable NIRS: the inability to conform comfortably to the head while maintaining robust signal quality in naturalistic environments.

Limitations

  • Single-participant validation limits generalizability
  • Separate-day comparisons with commercial devices introduce physiological variability
  • Intermittent LED failures from mechanical stress on flexible interconnects
  • Wired data collection used (BLE not yet implemented)

Future Directions

  • Wireless BLE operation — complete the wireless data transmission path
  • Power optimization — adaptive sampling, deeper AFE feature utilization for extended battery life
  • Multi-modal sensing — integrate accelerometers for motion artifact rejection; EEG electrodes for fusion
  • PCB refinement — improve interconnect durability under repeated bending
  • Expanded validation — diverse participants, hair-covered regions, ambulatory conditions
