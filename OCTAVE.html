Optical Coherence Tomography And Vibrometry Endoscope (OCTAVE)

A handheld scanner that sees and measures the middle ear to diagnose hearing loss
• NIH DEBUT Challenge 2nd Prize Winner • UC Riverside Senior Design • Dr. H. Park, Dr. W. Dong

Read the Story

What It Is

Conductive hearing loss affects tens of millions of people, but doctors have no good tool to see what inside the ear is actually broken. OCTAVE is a handheld probe that combines two jobs: high-resolution imaging of the middle-ear structures and measurement of how the tiny ear bones vibrate in response to sound. This lets a clinician pinpoint the problem in one visit, without exploratory surgery.

How It Works

A 1310 nm light source feeds a Michelson interferometer (the OCT engine) miniaturized into a ~0.6 kg probe. A MEMS mirror scans the beam; a GRIN lens focuses it through a standard otoscope tip. Doppler vibrometry measures ear-bone motion, and a built-in fiberscope shows the doctor where they're aiming — all through a custom real-time software interface.

My Role

Lead optomechanical designer on a 5-person team. I designed and manufactured the miniaturized mounts for most optical components that required persice optical alignment adjustment through handheld movement. I manufactured the components with μm-degree accuracy and were embedded onto a laser-cut platform that ensured optical alignment.

Key Achievements

  • NIH DEBUT Challenge 2nd Prize — nationally recognized undergraduate biomedical innovation
  • 49.9% optical throughput after alignment (broadband 1310 nm source)
  • SLA-printed mounts with <1° warping, ±50 µm positional tolerance
  • Real-time dual-window software: live OCT imaging + vibrometry controls
  • Validated on a human fingertip — visualizing a sweat gland and resolving skin layers on a μm resolution.
  • Patentable optomechanics: a fraction of the cost of commercial lab mounts
