Neuro-FLux

A fully flexible, wearable brain-monitoring device that tracks cerebral blood flow in real time
• Master's Thesis Research • Carnegie Mellon University • Advisor: Dr. Jana Kainerstorfer

Read the Story

What It Is

Latest update (July–December 2026) After finishing my M.S. in May 2026, I stayed on in the Biophotonics Lab as a Research Associate to keep moving Neuro-FLux forward. I integrated Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) wireless data streaming, and I'm refining the firmware with my Ph.D. mentor toward a field-deployable wearable.

Neuro-FLux is a wearable near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) device that measures blood oxygenation and flow in the brain continuously. Existing tools (EEG, fMRI) can't do this in everyday life, and earlier wearables lost signal the moment the wearer moved. Neuro-FLux solves that by putting the optics on an ultra-thin flexible printed circuit board (PCB) that bends to the shape of the head, keeping firm skin contact and clean optical coupling even during motion.

Why It Matters

Continuous cerebral monitoring helps catch neurological problems early and opens the door to personalized brain health. Neuro-FLux showed that research-grade optical measurement is possible in a comfortable, flexible patch — not just a bulky lab rig.

My Role

I owned the electrical and embedded stack. I selected the photodetector and tuned the analog front-end, designed the flexible PCB in Altium, and wrote the STM32 firmware in C (SPI + DMA sampling, I2C/UART, BLE streaming). I also built two object-oriented MATLAB pipelines, ran the validation against commercial systems, and wrote the thesis. When timing or signal quality broke, I used an oscilloscope and logic analyzer to tell hardware faults from firmware bugs.

Key Results

  • 36.14 dB SNR at heart rate, on par with a commercial CW-NIRS system (34.49 dB) under the same conditions
  • 100 Hz stable operation with optimized acquisition timing
  • ~2 dB SNR gain from sensor interfacing and front-end tuning
  • Agreement with a clinical FD-NIRS reference during breathing/hyperventilation tasks
  • Fully flexible form factor: 56.6 × 24.4 mm, ~0.3 mm thick polyimide board
  • TPU 3D-printed enclosure + silicone skin interface for comfort and stable coupling
  • Real-time tracking of heart-rate-driven brain blood-flow changes
  • BLE wireless streaming integrated (2026)
